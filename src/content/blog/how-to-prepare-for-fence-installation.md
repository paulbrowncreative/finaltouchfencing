Most fence delays have nothing to do with building the fence. They come from a property line question, a missing HOA approval or a buried line nobody knew about. Work through this fence installation checklist before the crew arrives.

## 1. Know where your property line is

The fence has to be on your property, or on the line with your neighbors’ agreement where the city allows it. Municipalities put this on you: Chesterfield Township and Macomb Township both say the homeowner or permit holder is responsible for identifying lot lines. If you’re unsure, your mortgage survey is a good start, and Chesterfield asks for one with the permit application. See [building a fence on the property line](/blog/fence-on-property-line/).

## 2. Talk to your neighbors

It’s courteous, and sometimes it’s required. In Harper Woods, a fence *on* the lot line needs written consent from all adjacent owners. If an old fence is coming out, agree on who owns it first. St. Clair Shores asks about this on its permit form and won’t determine ownership for you.

## 3. Check HOA or subdivision restrictions

Cities and townships don’t enforce private subdivision rules. Macomb Township notes that some subdivisions don’t allow fences at all, even though the township will issue a permit if the ordinance is met. Get HOA approval in writing before scheduling.

## 4. Get the fence permit

Most communities we serve require one. Fees, documents and timelines vary. Macomb Township, for example, runs a grade check and then takes seven to 10 working days to issue the permit. See the [fence permit guide](/blog/fence-permit-rules-macomb-county/) for your city.

## 5. Mark utilities through MISS DIG 811

Michigan law requires a MISS DIG 811 request at least **three business days** before digging. If a contractor does the digging, the contractor is responsible for placing that request. Digging must start within 14 days of the ticket’s start date, and anyone digging within 4 feet of a mark has to hand-dig to find the line.

## 6. Flag your private lines

Utilities mark only their own lines. **Private lines aren’t marked**, including:

- underground sprinkler lines and heads
- invisible or electric dog fence wire
- power or gas run to a shed, detached garage, pool or grill
- landscape lighting wire

Show us where these run during the estimate. If you’re not sure, a private locating company can mark them for a fee.

## 7. Clear the fence line

Before the crew arrives, move anything along the fence line: planters, play sets, firewood and vehicles parked on a side drive. Trim back branches and shrubs that overhang the line, and keep pets inside on installation days.

## 8. After the fence is built

- Some cities, including St. Clair Shores and Macomb Township, require a final inspection after the fence is built.
- New pressure-treated wood needs time to dry before it takes stain well. Schedule [fence staining](/services/fence-staining/) for later.
- Check gate latches after the first few weeks, while the posts settle.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who applies for the fence permit, me or the contractor?

It depends on the city. The St. Clair Shores application can be filed by the property owner or the contractor. Chesterfield Township’s application must be signed by the homeowner or a license holder. We’ll go over the process for your city at the estimate.

### Who calls MISS DIG 811?

Whoever does the digging. When we install your fence, we’re the excavator, so we’re responsible for the MISS DIG 811 request.

### What should I tell the crew about my yard?

Point out sprinkler lines, invisible dog fence wire, septic or drain lines, buried wiring and anything else underground that utilities won’t mark. Also mention soft spots, recent grading and where pets will be.

### How long will my yard be torn up?

Most of the work is digging post holes and setting posts along the fence line. Expect some disturbed soil and grass right along the line, which recovers quickly.
