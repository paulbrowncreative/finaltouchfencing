The best fence material depends on three things: what the fence is for, how much upkeep you want and what your city allows on your lot. Here’s how wood, vinyl, aluminum and chain link fences compare for homes in St. Clair Shores, Macomb County and Metro Detroit.

## Fence materials at a glance

| Material | Privacy | Upkeep | Up-front cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Wood](/services/wood-fencing/) | Full (privacy, shadow box) or none (picket, split rail) | Stain every few years | Moderate | Backyards, custom heights and styles |
| [Vinyl](/services/vinyl-fencing/) | Full | Rinse occasionally | Higher | Low-maintenance privacy |
| [Aluminum](/services/aluminum-fencing/) | None; open view | Minimal; doesn’t rust | Higher | Waterfront lots, front yards, pools |
| [Chain link](/services/chain-link-fencing/) | None | Minimal | Lowest | Dogs, back lots, large areas |

## Wood fences

Wood is the most flexible fence material. It can be cut to fit odd lot lines and slopes, built as a solid privacy fence, a two-sided shadow box fence, a picket fence or an open split rail fence, and stained any color. The trade-off is upkeep: plan on a cleaning and a coat of stain every few years to keep it from graying. See [when to stain a wood fence](/blog/when-to-stain-a-wood-fence/).

In Clay Township, the ordinance requires the side facing neighbors to be “finished,” for example stained or painted, which is one more reason to budget for stain.

## Vinyl fences

A vinyl fence gives you privacy without the maintenance. There’s no staining, painting or sealing, and most panels look the same from both sides, so you never argue over who gets the “good side.” Vinyl costs more up front than wood.

## Aluminum fences

Black ornamental aluminum looks like wrought iron but doesn’t rust. It’s open, so it doesn’t give privacy, and that’s exactly why it’s often the only option on waterfront lots. St. Clair Shores bans privacy fences on waterfront and canal lots, and Chesterfield Township allows only decorative aluminum or wrought iron (up to 48 inches) on the water side of Anchor Bay and Salt River lots. Details in our [waterfront fence guide](/blog/waterfront-canal-fence-rules/).

## Chain link fences

Chain link is the budget workhorse for dog yards and back property lines. It’s durable and see-through. Some ordinances don’t treat it as decorative: Clay Township excludes it from decorative front-yard fencing, and Chesterfield Township bans it from waterfront front yards.

## Which fence material lasts longest in Michigan?

Every material can last a long time if it’s installed well, and every material fails early if the posts are set too shallow. Beyond the posts:

- **Wood** lasts longest when it’s stained regularly and kept from sitting in wet soil or mulch.
- **Vinyl** doesn’t rot and doesn’t need finishing. Keep it clean and keep the gates adjusted.
- **Aluminum** doesn’t rust and needs very little care.
- **Chain link** is durable and low-maintenance. Keep the gate hinges and latches in good shape.

## Mixing materials on one property

Many yards use more than one material, and local rules often encourage it. A common combination: a 6-foot wood or vinyl privacy fence around the backyard and a low black aluminum or split rail fence in front, where solid fences generally aren’t allowed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What’s the cheapest type of fence?

Chain link usually costs the least per foot. Wood is typically next, and vinyl and aluminum cost more up front. See [what drives fence cost](/blog/fence-cost-factors/).

### What’s the most low-maintenance fence?

Vinyl and aluminum. Neither needs stain or paint.

### What’s the best fence for privacy?

A solid wood privacy fence, a wood shadow box fence or a vinyl privacy fence. See [shadow box vs. privacy fence](/blog/shadow-box-vs-privacy-fence/).

### What’s the best fence for dogs?

For most dogs, a 6-foot wood or vinyl privacy fence. See [the best fence for dogs](/blog/best-fence-for-dogs/).
